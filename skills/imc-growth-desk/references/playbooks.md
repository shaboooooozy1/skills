# Growth desk playbooks

## Daily action brief

Start with the decision: the three actions that matter most now. Use a short table: **account/person | verified change | why now | next action | due | source**. Rank hard deadlines and client blockers before speculative growth. Separate a recorded deadline from your suggested target date. Include a small **waiting on others** list only when it changes today's work. Close with a ready-to-use first step, such as the first two sentences of a draft.

When no private data is accessible, say so and produce a practical setup action rather than inventing three live priorities.

## Prospect signal card

- Company and location.
- Confirmed event, event date, source, and retrieval date.
- Relationship: client / prospect / referral / unknown, with evidence.
- Potential P&C exposure: an inference, not a confirmed gap.
- One relevant discovery question.
- One concrete action and suggested target date.

Suggested discovery prompts: new facilities → construction handoff and property values; acquisitions → acquired entities and program transition; fleet growth → vehicle schedules and safety controls; manufacturing changes → equipment dependencies and downtime; international operations → transit, territory, and local-policy questions. These are conversation topics, not coverage conclusions.

## Account brief

Use: **meeting objective**, **verified business facts**, **what changed**, **open questions**, **relationship map**, **three discovery questions**, **recommended next step**. Keep unverified insurance details in an explicit unknowns block. Do not invent names to complete a relationship map. Ask for existing policies or verified schedules only when required for actual coverage analysis.

## Referral and follow-up

Reconstruct who introduced whom, the purpose, latest commitment, and reply ownership. Draft with one specific reference to context, one benefit or useful question, and one clear request. Avoid assumed closeness, fabricated meetings, and unsupported savings claims. Show the intended recipient and subject; do not send automatically.

## Weekly growth review

Report outcomes and blockers before activity counts. For supplied snapshots compare opportunity IDs and consistent units. Separate additions/removals from stage progress. If only one snapshot exists, report the present state; do not manufacture a trend, velocity, win rate, or conversion rate. List the three opportunities requiring a decision, the top data gaps, and one next step per owner.

## Pipeline review

Use the JSON output for renewal windows and account concentration, in addition to Markdown's compact action report. Show missing-revenue counts and probability coverage beside monetary totals. Treat weighted revenue as a user-input scenario. Do not claim it is a calibrated forecast. The renewal queue covers open opportunities only; a renewal book requires separate current-policy records, including existing clients whose sales opportunity previously closed as won.

## Product experience defaults

Treat the natural-language skill as the primary interface. Offer the six modes by intent, not a long setup wizard. Show the top three actions before supporting detail; make evidence and unknowns easy to inspect. Use progressive disclosure for account detail and technical methods. Preserve empty, partial, stale, and failed-source states explicitly.

A separate web application is an additional implementation, not something this installed skill deploys. If requested, load Product Design for visual selection and implementation, preserve these evidence states, and use authenticated data access. Do not label static sample data a live command center.
