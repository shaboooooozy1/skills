# Loss Summary Narrative

The reader is the producer, then the client's CFO or an underwriter. They want three things: what happened, whether it is getting better or worse, and what to do.

## Structure (one page)

1. **Headline finding.** One sentence. The single most important fact, with its number.
   "Five claims drove 71% of incurred losses over five years. Four were auto."
2. **The record.** Two or three sentences: years covered, carriers, claim count, total incurred, loss ratio if premium was given, valuation dates.
3. **Trend.** Frequency (claim counts) and severity (average incurred) by year. Say which is moving. Flag immature years (under 24 months) every time you cite them.
4. **Drivers.** Large losses, top causes, and the line that concentrates the dollars. Name causes, never claimants.
5. **Open exposure.** Open claim count and outstanding reserves. Note any open claim with zero reserve.
6. **Data questions for the carrier.** From Data_Flags. One line each. Omit if none.
7. **What it means for the program.** Two to four bullets framed as questions to test, not conclusions. Examples:
   - "Frequency is low and stable. Test whether a higher deductible lowers total cost."
   - "Auto severity is the story. Test fleet controls and driver selection before marketing."
   - "Two years are immature. Get updated loss runs before the submission."
8. **Review gate** (verbatim from SKILL.md).

## Language

- "Incurred" = paid plus case reserves less recoveries, as valued. Never call it "ultimate."
- "Loss ratio" = incurred divided by earned premium. Say "written" if the user supplied written premium.
- Frequency = claim count. Severity = average incurred per claim.
- Do not say a reserve is too high or too low. Say "the reserve should be reviewed with the carrier."
- Do not compare to industry averages unless the user supplies the benchmark and its source.

## Immaturity

Losses in recent policy years keep developing as claims are reported and reserves change. When a year is flagged `immature`, write "as currently valued, [year] is immature. Its numbers may still change materially." Never apply development factors on your own.

## Zero-loss years

Say them out loud. "No reported claims in 2022" is a selling point to an underwriter. Confirm the loss runs cover that year before writing it.
