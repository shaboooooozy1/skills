# Voice Guide: Churchill-Hemingway Style Reference

## The Core Formula

Churchill's contribution: Weight. Authority. The sense that every word was chosen because no other word would do. Sentences that feel inevitable.

Hemingway's contribution: Economy. Rhythm. The courage to stop writing before the point is belabored. Trust the reader to be intelligent.

Combined effect: Prose that commands attention without raising its voice.

## Sentence Construction Rules

**Rule 1: Lead with the strongest word.**
- Bad: "There are several important factors to consider regarding your cyber exposure."
- Good: "Your cyber exposure has three gaps. Let me show you."

**Rule 2: Cut every word that doesn't earn its place.**
- Bad: "I wanted to take a moment to personally reach out and let you know about an upcoming event."
- Good: "Mark your calendar. January 15th. Dinner at The Erie Club."

**Rule 3: Use periods where others use commas.**
- Bad: "We specialize in commercial insurance, focusing on emerging risks like cyber liability, cargo coverage, and cryptocurrency insurance."
- Good: "We specialize in commercial insurance. Emerging risks, specifically. Cyber. Cargo. Cryptocurrency. The exposures most brokers haven't studied yet."

**Rule 4: End sentences with strong words, not prepositions or qualifiers.**
- Bad: "This is a topic that your business should be thinking about."
- Good: "Your business needs this conversation."

**Rule 5: Active voice. No exceptions in professional correspondence.**
- Bad: "Your renewal is being reviewed by our team and will be sent to you shortly."
- Good: "I am reviewing your renewal. Expect my recommendations Thursday."

## Opening Lines

The first sentence determines whether the rest gets read. Open with tension, value, or a direct statement.

**Event Invitations:**
- "You have worked hard. Your money should work harder."
- "Keep your evening open. January 15th. I have something worth your time."
- "This is not another dinner. This is a conversation that could change your tax strategy."

**Prospecting / Cold Outreach:**
- "Your fleet crosses state lines. Your coverage should keep up."
- "I reviewed your industry's loss data last week. One number stood out."
- "[Company name] is growing. Growth creates exposures. Some of yours may be uncovered."

**Renewal Reminders:**
- "Your policy renews in 60 days. The market has shifted. We should talk."
- "Renewal season. Three things have changed since last year that affect your premiums."

**Follow-Ups:**
- "I sent a note last Tuesday. The question still stands."
- "Two weeks ago I flagged a gap in your coverage. I'd like to close it before it costs you."

**Thank You / Post-Event:**
- "Thank you for joining us last evening. One topic stayed with me."
- "Good company. Good conversation. I meant what I said about your [specific topic]."

## Closing Lines

Every close should contain exactly one clear action with specifics.

**Strong closings:**
- "Does Thursday at 2:00 or Friday at 10:00 work for a 20-minute call?"
- "Reply to this email to confirm your seat. Space is limited."
- "I will follow up Tuesday. If you want to move faster, call me."
- "Let me know by Friday. I will hold the quote until then."

**Weak closings to avoid:**
- "Let me know if you have any questions." (Too passive. What question would they ask?)
- "Feel free to reach out at your convenience." (Their convenience is never.)
- "I look forward to hearing from you." (Hope is not a strategy.)
- "Don't hesitate to contact me." (Nobody was hesitating. They were ignoring.)

## Tone Calibration

**Existing clients:** Warm authority. You know their business. Speak with the confidence of someone who has earned trust. First names. References to past conversations or specific details about their operation.

**Prospects (warm):** Informed confidence. You have done your research. Reference something specific about their business — not a generic compliment, but a real observation.

**Prospects (cold):** Sharp value. Lead with what you know about their industry risk. No throat-clearing. No credentials paragraph. One compelling reason to reply, then stop.

**Event invitations:** Exclusive gravity. The reader should feel selected, not marketed to. Use "invite" not "would love to have you." Use "limited seating" not "RSVP required."

**Difficult conversations (premium increases, claim issues):** Direct honesty wrapped in competence. State the fact. Explain the cause. Present the path forward. No apologies for market conditions you don't control.

## Word Choice

| Instead of... | Write... |
|---|---|
| reach out | contact / call / write |
| touch base | follow up / check in |
| circle back | revisit / return to |
| leverage | use |
| synergy | alignment / fit |
| bandwidth | time / capacity |
| I just wanted to | [delete entirely — start with the real sentence] |
| I hope this finds you well | [delete entirely] |
| at your earliest convenience | by [specific date] |
| we would love to | we invite you to |
| don't hesitate | [replace with specific action] |
| a number of | [use the actual number] |
| in the near future | by [date] / this week / this month |
| I think | [delete — just state the thing] |
| I believe | [delete — just state the thing] |
| I feel like | [delete — just state the thing] |
